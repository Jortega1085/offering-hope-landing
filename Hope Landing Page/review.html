About this Keynote i

Most men do not have language for what they are carrying. The body, however, has been speaking the whole time — chronic tension, insomnia, rage, the back that won't loosen, the jaw that won't unclench, the gut that won't settle.

Built To Break is the keynote about what your body has been saying that your mind would not let it say. Sixty to ninety minutes, a live somatic exercise included, and an opening question — hands raised — that gets the whole room participating in the first sixty seconds.

There will be a formal land acknowledgment before the personal story. Ceremony is woven into the work, not added to it. Healing is not something you find in a clinic. It is something you remember.

The Method ii
Pillar one

Somatic Awareness

Reading what your body is carrying — the tension, the sleep that won't come, the rage with nowhere to land — before it gets any louder.

Pillar two

Mental Awareness

Recognizing the old agreements quietly running your decisions. The ones you didn't sign but keep paying for. Naming the spell so you can break it.

Pillar three

Intuitive Awareness

Telling the difference between fear dressed as wisdom and your gut telling the truth. Trusting the signal that was always there.

What You'll Leave With iii
A clearer name for the pattern your body has been carrying.
The Three Pillars framework — somatic, mental, intuitive awareness — and how each one keeps you stuck.
One live somatic practice you can use the same week.
A grounded first step — not a shortcut, not a fix, an actual beginning.
Your Guide iv

Hope Kimple

Intuitive Life Coach · Somatic Practitioner

Hope is the founder of Hands of Hope Wellness Center in Hayward, California, with 16+ years of clinical experience guiding people through grief, transition, and the slow, brave work of coming back to themselves.

Hope is Lakota (Rosebud) and was raised in Comanche ways. She carries two nations in her body and two traditions of understanding the world. Every keynote opens with a land acknowledgment, and ceremony is woven into her practice — not added as decoration. Healing is not something you find in a clinic. It is something you remember.
